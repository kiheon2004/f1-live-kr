# F1 team pit wall stands ("pit gantry" / "prat perch"), 2025–2026: research notes

Researched 2026-10-16. **S** = stated by the source. **I** = my inference (not stated anywhere). Confidence: H/M/L.

**Overall:** No source found gives physical dimensions (width, depth, height, platform height) for any team's stand. Teams and the FIA do not publish them. Every dimension below is "not found", and any numbers used in a 3D model must be treated as artistic estimates.

---

## 1. Typical construction

- **S: what it is.** A "command room" set up on the pit wall for race use. It seats roughly 3 to 6 people side by side ("3〜6名ほど"). The team director, engineers and strategists check data on built-in monitors and PCs and relay information to drivers by radio and pit boards. Source: Honda glossary "Pit Wall Stand", https://global.honda/jp/tech/motorsports/Formula-1/glossary/terms/Pit_Wall_Stand/ (undated). **Confidence: H** (for the general description).
- **S: how it is mounted.** "Structures that are built along the top of the chemin de ronde (wall-walk), against the fence between the pitlane and the main straight." The wall itself has small openings onto the main straight for pit boards. Source: Autosport, R. Braybrook, "Everything you need to know about the pitwall in F1", edited 2024-03-19, https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-pitwall-in-f1-who-sits-there-and-what-does-it-do/10588720/. **H**
  - **I:** The stand sits on top of and against the pit wall, faces the pit lane and garages, and has its back to the debris fence and the main straight. People seated there face the garage. Ferrari's seat list is given "left to right, as seen from the garage" (F1.com, 2020). **M**
- **S: an elevated platform.** The FIA circuit rules (Appendix O 2026, §7.9) call for the pit lane to be "separated by a pit wall and signalling platform" from the starting straight. The International Sporting Code Appendix H restricts the "pit wall signalling platform" to officials and team personnel holding a special pass. Sources: https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/appendix_o_2026_published_23122025.pdf (published 2025-12-23), and Appendix H as quoted in search results. **H**
  - **I:** The platform is the circuit's permanent signalling platform on top of the wall. Team stands are bolted or placed onto it. **M**
- **S: an enclosure that protects against the weather.** The Haas stand has "a surrounding enclosure that protects engineers from the elements", is assembled, disassembled and shipped around the world several times a year, and has "about a dozen TV screens". The article describes a typical pit wall as having comfortable stools, a tabletop, smartphone charging outlets and "a dozen or so screens". Source: The Drive, 2023-02-24, https://www.thedrive.com/news/haas-f1s-new-baby-pit-wall-will-save-250k-for-car-development. **M** (a general-audience outlet)
- **S: canopy.** Mercedes has a "pit wall canopy" carrying sponsor branding (Nasdaq, from Miami 2025 onward). Source: F1Technical, 2025-05-15, https://www.f1technical.net/news/26516. **H** that a branded canopy exists.
  - **I:** Most or all team stands have a roof or canopy carrying team livery and sponsor logos. This is consistent with photos generally, but no source states it for every team. The 1/20 Fujimi model kit of a "F1 Pit Wall Stand" includes "monitors, radios, chairs, and roof" (https://www.hlj.com/1-20-scale-pit-crew-c-set-f1-pit-wall-stand-fuj11332). **M**
- **S: seats.** Red Bull's "Bulls' Guide To: The Pitwall" says teams typically fit "between four and eight seats", "each position having a stool, intercom panel and monitor". This is quoted via search snippets because the page is JS-only and could not be fetched. Undated. https://www.redbullracing.com/int-en/projects/bulls-guide-to-the-pit-wall. **M**
  - Other sources give similar ranges:
    - Autosport (2024): "around three to eight seats". **H**
    - F1.com (2020-02-04): "some teams use a core of four, others up to seven". **H**
    - RaceFans (2023-02-24): "around five to seven personnel". **H**
    - GPFans (2023-02-27): a regular pit wall has "six to eight positions". **M**
- **S: seat type.** Aston Martin uses bespoke pit wall *stools* by Formitalia, with a "high precision cast aluminium structure" and leather upholstery. They are designed for race engineers and strategists. Undated (page footer 2026). https://formitalia.it/blogs/news/aston-martin-formula-one-team-pit-wall. **H** for Aston Martin. **I:** High stools rather than office chairs are the norm. **M**
- **S: monitors and comms.**
  - Staff sit before "a bank of monitors" showing car telemetry, FIA timing, TV and GPS-tracked car video, and weather radar. Source: Autosport, Pat Symonds, 2020-05-01, https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/what-really-happens-on-an-f1-pitwall-5113836/5113836/.
  - Each person configures their own screens. Communication runs over intercom, which garage and factory staff also join. Source: F1.com, 2020-02-04.
  - Monitors can be interchanged per session (Autosport, 2024).
  - Number of screens per seat: **not found**. The Haas stand has "about a dozen" screens for 3–6 seats (The Drive, 2023). **I:** That works out to roughly 2–4 screens per seat, likely two rows (upper and lower) on a console. **L**
- **Rows:** **not found** stated. **I:** Photos and every description point to a single row of seats side by side ("side by side", per Honda). **M**
- **Width, depth, height, platform height above the pit lane: not found.** The only size signals:
  - Haas' 3-seat unit was a "50 per cent" cut versus 6 seats.
  - The 3-seat unit saved "400 kilos in freight". GPFans, 2023-02-27, https://www.gpfans.com/en/f1-news/102152/haas-f1-pit-wall-prat-perch-benefit/. **M**
  - **I:** A 6-seat stand therefore weighs at least about 800 kg of freight. **L**
- **Access (steps/ladder), railings: not found.** Red Bull's guide (snippet) notes that gates and holes in the pit wall are kept to a minimum for safety. **L**
- **Headsets: S.** Pit wall staff use monitors, comms channels and headsets (Red Bull guide snippet). **M**
- **Umbrellas: not found** in any source.
- **Nickname:** "the prat perch". Getty caption, McLaren pit wall gantry, 2004 (https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/detail/news-photo/the-mclaren-pit-wall-gantry-aka-the-prat-perch-formula-one-news-photo/2179552318). **H**
- **Freight:**
  - Pat Symonds called the stand "an anachronism" and campaigned to remove it to save freight weight (Autosport, 2020).
  - The stands are team-owned and shipped to every race (Haas 2025: "transport the larger pit wall box to all 24 races", Autosport, 2025-02-26). **H**

    3. Mark Lowe (sporting director)
    4. Francesco Nenci (chief engineer)
    5. Carine Cridelich (head of strategy)
  - One further seat is reserved for Gene Haas, or an extra engineer when he is absent.
  - History: 2023–2024 was a 3-seat gantry, used to save about $250k a year in freight.
    - 2023: Steiner, Komatsu, Crolla (F1i 2023-02-24, https://f1i.com/news/466667-steiner-explains-haas-downsized-pit-wall-gantry.html).
    - 2024: Komatsu, Crolla and a race strategist.
  - 2026 seat count: **not found**. **I:** Probably still 6. **L**

  1. Leclerc race engineer
  2. head of track operations
  3. head of race strategy
  4. chief race engineer
  5. sporting director
  6. team principal
  7. second race engineer

  For 2025/2026 the role holders reported to Vasseur were Diego Ioverno (sporting director, from 2023), Ravin Jain (strategy, from 2023) and Matteo Togninalli (head of track engineering). Sources: Flashscore/ESPN 2023. The 2025/2026 seat count was **not found**. **I:** Likely still about 7. **M** for the structure, **L** for the count.
- **Mercedes:**
  - Has a branded pit wall canopy and a separate "engineers' central island station" in the garage. Source: F1Technical, 2025-05-15. **H**
  - Toto Wolff sits in the garage, not on the pit wall (Autosport, 2024). **H**
  - The pit wall is explained by sporting director Ron Meadows in a team video (F1-fansite, 2020-08-11). **M**
  - Seat count: **not found**.
- **Red Bull:**
  - Christian Horner sat on the pit wall (Autosport, 2024). Horner left in July 2025 and Laurent Mekies took over. **I:** Mekies' position on the wall is not verified. **L**
  - The team introduced a "new pit wall" in 2021 (F1 TV Tech Talk, Craig Scarborough, 2021-09-06, https://www.formula1.com/en/video/free-preview-f1-tv-tech-talk-red-bulls-new-pit-wall.1710164956205236054). The design details sit behind the F1 TV paywall: **not found**.
  - The Drive (2023) shows Red Bull's unit as "much larger" than Haas' 3-seater. **M**
- **McLaren:** Andrea Stella (team principal) is on the pit wall during races in 2025–2026 (pitdebrief, 2025/2026). Seat count: **not found**. **M**
- **Aston Martin:** custom Formitalia cast-aluminium leather stools with embroidered logos (undated). Seat count: **not found**. **H** for the stools.
- **Alpine, Racing Bulls, Williams, Sauber/Audi, Cadillac (2026):** no team-specific pit wall description found. RaceFans' 2023 Bahrain gallery has pit gantry photos of all ten teams, but no counts. **Not found.**
- **Team colours:** **I:** Each stand carries team livery and sponsors (the Mercedes canopy with Nasdaq branding is confirmed). **M**

## 3. Rules (FIA)

- **2025 Sporting Regulations, Art. 26.12:** "Only twelve (12) team members for each Competitor are permitted in the signalling area during any free practice session, qualifying session, sprint qualifying session, sprint session and the race." Source: FIA 2025 F1 SR Issue 5, 2025-04-30, https://api.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2025_formula_1_sporting_regulations_-_issue_5_-_2025-04-30.pdf. **H**
- **2026 Sporting Regulations (Section B), Art. B1.5.8:** the same 12-person limit. Source: FIA 2026 Section B Issue 5, 2026-02-27, https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_05_-_2026-02-27.pdf. **H**
- **Race start:**
  - 2025, Art. 44.13: during the race start, the pit wall must be kept free of everyone except the team personnel permitted under 26.12, officials and fire marshals.
  - 2026, Art. B5.1.8: the same, worded "start of any TTCS". **H**
- **Fast lane:** the lane closest to the pit wall is the "fast lane", no more than 3.5 m wide. Sources: 2025 Art. 34.4; 2026 definitions. **H**
  - **I:** The stand is therefore separated from the garages by the fast lane, at most 3.5 m wide, plus the inner (working) lane. **M**
- **Restricted areas:** the signalling area is defined as a restricted area for personnel-exemption purposes (2026, Art. B9.4). **H**
- **Garage allocation:** the FIA allocates garages and pit-lane areas "on an equal basis" (2026, Art. B1.6.1). Teams may not leave equipment in the fast lane. **H**
- **Appendix O (2026, §7.9):** the pit lane should be at least 12 m wide, adjacent to the starting straight, "separated by a pit wall and signalling platform". The "pit wall debris fence" construction specification must be submitted. **H**
- **Debris fence:** climbing the pit wall debris fences is forbidden at any time (race director event notes, 2023, reported by PlanetF1, Autosport and Crash.net). **H**
- **Maximum size or dimension limits for team pit wall stands: not found** in the 2025 or 2026 Sporting Regulations (text searched) or Appendix O. **I:** Stand size is limited in practice by the circuit's signalling platform space and by the cost cap and freight costs, not by an explicit rule. **M**

## 4. Placement along the pit lane

- **S: Placement.** The wall is a "track-side island" overlooking the pit lane and the garage (F1.com, 2020). **H**
  - **I:** Each team's stand sits on the pit wall roughly opposite its own garage, in the same order as the garages, so the team can see its own pit box. This is consistent with Ferrari describing seat order "as seen from the garage", but no source states it explicitly. **M**
  - Offset, if any: **not found**.
- **S: Monaco exception.** There is no room on the pit wall. Teams put their pit wall stand on the upper floor of the pit building, above the garage.
  - Horner: "Here in Monaco, the pit wall is upstairs and the garage is downstairs" (GrandPrix247, 2016).
  - A Ferrari engineer: "even our usual pit wall stand is up there" (via search results, f1technical "logistical challenges of Monaco").
  - Red Bull's "Bulls' Guide to Monaco" says the same.

  **H**
- **S: team-supplied stands.** Team stands are team-owned freight shipped to every race (Haas 2023/2025 articles; Symonds 2020). Teams run multiple sea-freight sets of garage kit (Aston Martin, 2026, https://www.astonmartinf1.com/en-GB/news/feature/shipping-to-sepang). A secondary blog lists "pit-wall" in the priority air/express freight tier (wcshipping.com). **H** that stands are team-supplied; **L** for the freight mode.
- **Circuit-provided fixed structures (Silverstone, Abu Dhabi, etc.): not found.** Circuits provide the wall, the signalling platform and the debris fence (Appendix O). At Silverstone the pit lane is "neither parallel to the pit straight nor at the same elevation" (Red Bull guide snippet). Crash.net reported "Silverstone gets new pit wall" (undated in results). **I:** Even at circuits with permanent covered platforms, teams still install their own console or gantry units. **L**
- **Singapore 2017:** the pit wall was moved about 275 mm further from the garages (FIA event document). This shows that wall position is circuit-specific. **H**
