### 2026 control changes (rules)
- **DRS removed.** Active aero switches between Straight mode (low drag, in FIA zones, no 1-second rule) and Corner mode (default, high downforce). It reverts automatically on braking or lift. Sources: [The Race](https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/boost-overtake-mode-active-aero-recharge-key-2026-terms-explained), [F1.com key terms](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/explained-the-new-key-terms-for-formula-1s-new-for-2026-rules.3T5BU6TC9quGcIpGzoWkY0), [McLaren](https://www.mclaren.com/racing/formula-1/2026/introducing-new-f1-terminology/).
- **Boost** button: the old max-power button, renamed. Usable anywhere if there is charge. The FIA capped race boost at +150 kW after Bearman's Suzuka crash; Komatsu: "He hit the boost button". Sources: [GPFans](https://www.gpfans.com/en/f1-news/1075966/f1-boost-button/), [Crash.net 2026-03-30](https://www.crash.net/f1/news/1092651/1/ollie-bearman-gives-first-reaction-scary-suzuka-f1-crash-fia-responds).
- **Overtake mode** (manual override): only when within 1 s at the detection point; +0.5 MJ on the next lap. The button is a request that the ECU validates (F1 Chronicle).
- **Recharge:** mostly automatic. The driver controls lift-off regen.
- No source gives the physical position of Boost or Overtake on any team's wheel.

### How drivers hold the wheel (sourced)
- Hands stay on the side silicone grips, roughly quarter-to-three. "9-and-3" is never stated verbatim but follows from the side grips, and Russell rests his palms on the lower grip. Frequently used controls sit under the thumbs: diff thumb rotaries/buttons near the top inner corners of the grips, N/PL buttons nearby. Lower-face rotaries need a hand taken off the grip. Sources: [motorsport.tech Williams wheel, 2020-11-06](https://motorsport.tech/formula-1/master-control-understanding-the-williams-fw-43-steering-wheel); [Ferrari SF90 wheel](https://www.ferrari.com/en-BG/magazine/articles/ferrari-sf90-formula-one-steering-wheel) ("where the driver's thumbs lie ... DIF IN / DIF MID"); [The Race 2025-07-15](https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/what-is-behind-george-russell-f1-steering-wheel-change/).
- **Paddles behind:** shift paddles at the forefinger tips (right = up, left = down). Clutch paddles (a mirrored pair) sit lower. Some teams add an extra upper or side paddle tier (multi-function, formerly DRS). Sources: motorsport.tech 2020; The Drive 2025.
- **Steering lock:** no authoritative wheel-rotation figure exists. Road-wheel steer is about 14° normally and about 20-22° for the Monaco hairpin ([F1.com 2015-05-24](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/ferrari-sf15-t-monaco-steering-modifications.2VI1MigPJ4tIvcrdtRNiiw)). Forum figures for hand rotation conflict. Suggestion: clamp the viewer at about ±90-120° (inference, not sourced).

## 4. Summary
- **Solid:** every wheel uses the FIA-standard Motion Applied electronics; the classic display is 4.3" at about 16:9, mounted centrally. Ferrari 2026 is slimmer, with a wavy top and 3 lower rotaries instead of 6 (Haas very likely shares it). Cadillac is a refaced Motion Applied TSW-700 (12 buttons, 3 rotaries, 6 thumbwheels, 15 top shift LEDs). McLaren 2026 adds a second small hybrid display above the gear readout. Mercedes 2025 rotaries are coloured per driver (Russell blue, Antonelli tricolore). The Alpine A525 replica gives the only real dimensions (284x190 mm, w/h ≈ 1.5).
- **Unknown from text:** Red Bull, Racing Bulls, Aston Martin and Audi are essentially undocumented. No source gives grip colours, button hex colours or exact per-side counts for any team.
