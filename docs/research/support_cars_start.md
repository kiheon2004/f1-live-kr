## 2. Where the cars wait

| Phase | Safety car | Medical car |
|---|---|---|
| Before the pit exit opens for reconnaissance laps (−40 min) | Leaves the pit lane and **parks at the front of the grid** until the 5-minute signal. It then laps the track "and take[s] up position", meaning its standby spot. **[src]** 2025 SR Art. 55.2; 2026 SR B5.2.1 | — |
| Formation lap | Not on track, unless the start is behind the SC (Art. 49 / B5.10). In that case it leads from the grid on the green gantry lights. | **[obs]** It trails the field and stops **behind the last row**. Wikipedia (Standing start): "the Medical Car positions itself behind the pack". McLaren F1 Playbook: lights sequence starts "once the grid has formed … and the medical car is in position at the back of the grid". Kym Illman, 31 Mar 2025: "with the medical car positioned at the rear, a marshal waves a green flag". No regulation text covers this. |
| Start / lap 1 | Waits at its parking position at the **end of the pit lane (pit exit)**. Mayländer (Motorsport.com interview): "we wait in the same parking position at the end of the pitlane, completely dressed, helmets on". A 2024 Aston release says it takes "its place at the exit of the pit lane awaiting instructions". | F1.com glossary K–O: "At the start of a race, the Medical Car lines up behind the grid and follows around the first lap in case of any incidents." F1.com medical car feature: it "follows behind the pack on Lap 1 … [then] peels back into the pits having not been needed". So it **enters the pit lane at the end of lap 1** and stands by for the rest of the race. |
| During the race | Parked at pit exit with the engine running (Mayländer, Motor1). Joins "regardless of where the leader is" with orange lights on (55.6 / B5.13.1). Returns via pit entry; orange lights go off in the "SC IN THIS LAP" lap (55.15). | Standby in the pit lane. Deploys on Race Control instruction, usually under red flag or SC. |

Raceteq notes that during start-procedure rehearsals the SC driver takes pole and the medical car driver P2. That is a rehearsal arrangement, not the race layout.

## 3. Start procedure clauses

The documents and their clause numbers:
- 2025 FIA F1 Sporting Regulations, Issue 5 (30 Apr 2025): https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2025_formula_1_sporting_regulations_-_issue_5_-_2025-04-30.pdf
- 2026 F1 Regulations Section B [Sporting]: Issue 09 (01 Oct 2026) is the current one; Issue 04 (10 Dec 2025) is at https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_04_-_2025-12-10_0.pdf

The 2026 rules are renumbered (Article B5 "TTCS"). The start procedure is **Art. 44 in 2025** and **B5.5–B5.7 in 2026**. Art. 48 in 2025 is not the start article.

| Topic | 2025 SR | 2026 SR (Section B) | Wording (abridged quotes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC at front of grid pre-race | 55.2 | B5.2.1 | "the safety car will leave the pit lane and take up position at the front of the grid and remain there until the five (5) minute signal is given. At this point … it will cover a lap of the track and take up position." |
| Countdown signals | 44.3–44.6 | B5.5 | 10/5/3/1 min and 15 s. At 10 min "everybody except drivers, officials and team technical staff must leave the grid". At 1 min engines start and all team personnel leave by 15 s. |
| Formation lap start | 44.7 | B5.6 | "When the green lights on the start gantry are illuminated, all cars … should begin the formation lap with the pole position driver leading." |
| Car stalled on grid | 44.12 | B5.7.1 | "The marshals alongside the grid, responsible for that row, must immediately wave a yellow flag and/or activate the yellow grid light panel". The car is pushed into the pit lane inner lane. |
| Standing start / lights | 44.10 a–b | B5.7.2 a–b | 2025: "a standing start, the signal being given by means of lights activated by the permanent starter. Once all the cars have come to a halt the five (5) second light will appear followed by the four, three, two and one second lights … the race will be started by extinguishing all red lights." 2026 (Issue 09): "Once all the F1 Cars have come to a halt, the Grid Light Panels will be illuminated blue (flashing) for five (5) seconds and the message 'Pre-Start' will be displayed on the start gantry, after which the first red light will be illuminated followed sequentially by the second … fifth red lights", with 1 s intervals. The hold after the fifth light is "at the sole discretion of the permanent starter". |
| Officials list | Sporting regs list ("A Permanent Starter", "A Medical Car Driver") | B1 officials list (same entries) | The FIA-appointed **permanent starter** runs the lights from the start control box at the line. Kym Illman names Rebecca Lee as the F1 starter since 2023, succeeding Christian Brill. |
| Formation behind SC | 49.1–49.3 | B5.10 | SC orange lights on at 10 min (wet tyres mandated) or 5 min. "When the green lights on the start gantry are illuminated the safety car will leave the grid and all drivers must follow in grid order". |
| Standing start after SC laps | 51 | B5.10 | "STANDING START" message, light panels show "SS", SC lights out, SC enters the pits. Cars return to the grid and follow the standing-start procedure, including lights. |
| Rolling start | 52 | B5.10 | "RS" on the panels. "As the safety car is approaching the pit entry road the FIA light panels will be extinguished and a green flag and/or green light panel will be displayed at the Line." **No start lights.** The race starts when the leader crosses the Line after the SC has pitted. |
| Red flag resumption | 58.6, 58.10–58.12 | B5.15 | Cars leave the pit lane behind the SC (orange lights on at 5 min). The SC enters the pits after 1 lap unless conditions require more. Standing-start option (58.11): the SC may stay in the pit lane, or pull aside so cars overtake it, and cars line up on the grid for a lights start. Rolling-start option (58.12): green flag or light at the Line as the SC approaches pit entry. |

### The green flag at the back of the grid

- **It is not written in the F1 Sporting Regulations.** Neither 2025 Art. 44 nor 2026 B5.7 mentions it. The rules say only "once all the cars have come to a halt". The flag is standard grid-marshal practice under the FIA marshal guidance and the race director's event notes, and it is the signal that tells race control the grid has come to a halt.
- What it means: after the formation lap, when the last car is stationary in its box and the medical car is behind the field, an official at the back of the grid waves a green flag. That tells the race director and permanent starter that the grid is complete. In 2026 the blue pre-start panels then flash for 5 s, then the red lights come on.
  - Motorsport.com flag guide: the green flag is "waved at the back of the grid at the start of the race to confirm all the cars have lined up." https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/insider-guide-f1-flag-rules/7088025/
  - Red Bull flag guide: "just before lights-out, a marshal behind the starting grid runs from one side of the track to the other with the green flag. This signals that the start procedure can begin." https://www.redbull.com/au-en/formula-1-flags-explained
  - Kym Illman, 31 Mar 2025: "As the field is set, with the medical car positioned at the rear, a marshal waves a green flag." https://www.kymillman.com/blog/the-secret-button-that-starts-every-f1-race/
  - Wikipedia (Racing flags): it is waved "at the rear of the grid following the formation lap to indicate to the official controlling the race start that the final car has reached its starting position".
- **Where the official stands:** on the track behind the last grid row, in front of the medical car. They cross the track from one side to the other while waving, then step off at the side. Lights come on only after the flag, so the official is clear of the track before the start. The run across and exit come from video and guides **[obs]**, not from the rules.
- **FIA Appendix H 2026, Art. 2.5.5 g** (FIA Safety Bulletin 56, "2026 Regulatory Changes", copy at https://www.autourheilu.fi/site/assets/files/49408/fia_safety_bulletin_56.pdf, fetched 2026-10-27): "The green flag marshal must now stand behind a barrier opening at the back of the grid, if available. If no barrier is present, the marshal must stand as far from the track edge as possible and remain alert to oncoming cars." This is the general marshalling recommendation for all FIA events. The user's observation of 2026 F1 starts (v0.46 request §4) is different: the marshal waves **on the track, on the medical car's side, near the medical car**, then steps away before the lights, never in the pit lane. The app follows the user for where the flag is waved and uses the barrier opening only for the step off (support.js flagSpot). Confidence on the exact spot (1.2 m in front of the medical car's nose, 0.6 m out from its side): L, an estimate.
- Rolling starts and restarts behind the SC: there is no rear-of-grid flag. The green flag or green light panel is shown **at the Line** (52.2 / 55.15 / 58.12).
- Formation lap: only the race cars run from the grid; the SC is already at pit exit. The medical car trails the field and parks behind the grid **[obs]**. On lap 1 the medical car follows the field and pits at the end of the lap **[src F1.com]**.

## 4. Data: are SC or medical car positions in the feed?

**The safety car: yes, but only while it is deployed. The medical car: no.**

- The public docs do not cover it. The FastF1 docs (https://docs.fastf1.dev/core.html, `Session.pos_data`) describe position data only "by car number". SC information is documented only through `track_status` and race-control messages. I found no FastF1 or OpenF1 documentation of an SC entry.
- `livetiming.formula1.com/static/...` returned **HTTP 401** from here today for 2024, 2025 and 2026, so I could not re-check the archive live.
- **Empirical check of 55 cached `Position.z` sessions in the app's own cache** (2024 British, all 2025 races cached, 2026 to Singapore):
  - Besides the driver numbers, `Position[].Entries` contains the keys **"241", "242", "243"**, with fields X/Y/Z/Status like a race car. Exactly **one** of the three carries real coordinates in a given session. The other two stay at `0,0,0` with `Status:"OffTrack"`. This matches Mercedes supplying 3 SC units per event.
  - The keys appear **only in sessions whose TrackStatus contains SC ("4")**: 21 of 21 SC sessions, including sprints. They never appear in sessions with only VSC or red flags, or no SC at all (33 sessions). The one exception is 2026 Monaco, whose cached Position file is truncated (8k samples).
  - Coordinates begin when the SC is deployed and end when it is back in the pit lane. Examples: 2025 Bahrain 241 at 15:56–16:03 UTC; 2025 Spain 243 at 14:17–14:28; 2025 Belgium 242 at 14:21–14:31, the delayed wet start behind the SC; 2025 Australia 242 for its three SC periods. **It is not transmitted while the SC is parked at pit exit or at the front of the grid before the start.** The waiting position therefore has to be modelled, for example at pit exit.
  - `CarData.z` has no 241–243 entries, so there is no speed, gear or other telemetry for the SC. `DriverList` has no SC entries.
  - **No medical car tag at all.** No extra number moves on lap 1 in any race, so medical car and rear-of-grid official positions must be simulated: behind the last grid box at the start, following the field on lap 1, into the pit lane at the end of lap 1.
- Practical mapping: treat any of `241/242/243` that has non-zero XYZ as "SAFETY CAR". Do not infer the manufacturer from the number.
